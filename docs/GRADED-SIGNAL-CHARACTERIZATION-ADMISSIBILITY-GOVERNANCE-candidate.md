## 1. Purpose and scope

This record materializes Ryan's seven accepted governance resolutions for the
admissibility of a possible future graded-signal characterization study. It
defines which origin, lifecycle, temporal-history, and case-surface changes
may remain within the characterization identity and which values must wait for
a later executable protocol.

It does not select a characterization family, specify cases, or create
execution authority. The characterization remains distinct from the completed
Stage 1A and Stage 1B work.

## 4. Ryan-governed characterization resolutions

### 4.1 Real adoption

Every characterization commitment must have an explicit earlier Luna adoption
event. This does not require a separate API session or request for every
commitment.

Auditable batching may be permitted only when all of the following hold:

- the mapping is frozen before calls;
- every commitment has an auditable adoption record;
- no later outcome or state information is available;
- no truth or label leakage is possible; and
- the batching arrangement preserves the required cross-session isolation.

### 4.2 Origin isolation

The characterization must preserve genuine prospective control by carrying
forward these semantic protections:

- no truth labels;
- no calibration scores;
- no selected thresholds;
- no future observed event or state;
- no eventual positive or negative outcome leakage;
- no transcript or original-conversation carry-forward;
- no hidden session state available to later scoring; and
- persistence carries only authorized state.

Stage-1A's exact five-session allocation, one-structure-per-session mapping,
twelve-commitment allocation, and all-five-before-calibration sequencing do
not transfer automatically by precedent. Their non-transfer is an explicit
characterization governance decision; no replacement arrangement is selected
here.

### 4.3 Origin prompt and adoption contract

This characterization deliberately adopts the exact Stage-1A origin prompt and
the exact Stage-1A adoption-response contract unchanged. Their use here was
not previously immutable characterization authority and is newly governed by
Ryan in this record. This choice avoids introducing a needless new
origin-elicitation variable.

This resolution adopts the prompt and contract only. It does not authorize
origin calls or decide their allocation, batching, count, or execution
discipline.

### 4.4 Origin-session allocation

The characterization does not inherit Stage-1A's five origin sessions, one
familiar structure per session, or twelve commitments per session. A later
executable protocol must freeze an auditable characterization-appropriate
origin allocation after its case surface is defined.

Batching may be allowed only under the real-adoption and origin-isolation rules
above. No concrete count, session allocation, or batching scheme is authorized
by this record.

### 4.5 Lifecycle boundary

Lifecycle vocabulary may vary only when the variation preserves the existing
prospective-control applicability semantics. Finite, mechanically defined
terminology or representations are permissible in characterization space only
when they preserve the same lifecycle role and truth semantics.

A new causal lifecycle dimension, a new state-machine mechanism, or a change to
what applicability means is outside this characterization identity. This
resolution selects no actual lifecycle vocabulary.

### 4.6 Temporal-history boundary

A later characterization surface may represent finite, complete, mechanically
truth-able temporal history inside the existing five-field interface:
`commitment`, `trigger_condition`, `prior_state`, `observed_event`, and
`lifecycle_state`.

The current identity does not admit a sixth model-visible history field, hidden
transcript or history, out-of-band model memory, unbounded prior conversation,
or non-persisted hidden session state. Temporal representation must remain
prospective-control applicability rather than becoming a generic
sequence/state-machine task. No actual temporal encoding is selected here.

### 4.7 Case-surface / invariant meta-boundary

Ordinary case-surface variation is permitted only inside the frozen
characterization identity. A proposed change is not ordinary case-surface
variation if it alters:

- the exact frozen Luna model or configuration;
- the exact frozen scoring-prompt bytes;
- the frozen score-response contract;
- mechanically established external truth or truth independence;
- prospective-control applicability as the construct; or
- the authorized five-field scorer interface.

Such a change cannot inherit this characterization identity and requires a
separately governed study from first principles.

## 6. Resulting admissibility consequence

The governed boundaries leave a conditional path by which at least one
surviving A-like or B-like geometry could later qualify for protocol synthesis
without changing the characterization identity. This is only a reachability
consequence of the boundaries.

No family is selected. The result is not evidence that A or B is clearly
viable, and it is not protocol, case-generation, or execution authorization.

# Graded-Signal Characterization Design-Space Conclusion

**Arc:** `GRADED_SIGNAL_CHARACTERIZATION_DESIGN_SPACE`
**Status:** **COMPLETED DESIGN-SPACE CONCLUSION — NON-EXECUTION RECORD**
**Canonical base:** `d629fdfab4901a1daa3a71a2d46bad06c84f2032`

This document records the completed scientific conclusion of the design-space
arc. It is not an executable protocol, governance amendment, Stage 1A or
Stage 1B authority, characterization execution authority, case-generation
authority, or architecture authority.

## 1. Arc purpose

The arc tested whether a future characterization surface could impose greater
inferential burden on mechanically truth-able prospective-control judgments
without merely adding cosmetic sophistication or changing the measured
construct.

## 2. Inherited boundary

The characterization identity inherits five immutable scientific invariants:

1. the exact frozen Stage-1A Luna model/configuration;
2. the exact frozen scoring-prompt bytes;
3. the exact frozen score-response contract;
4. mechanically established external truth, independently determined from the
   tested model; and
5. prospective-control applicability as the scored construct.

Only a later, separately preregistered case surface may vary. These invariants
are inherited from the ratified graded-signal characterization addendum and
are not amended by this record.

## 3. Why the original surface was insufficient

Stage-1A contained 20 logical configurations. Each familiar structure had one
positive logical configuration, and the observed score support was endpoint-only:
`{0,1}`.

A harder-looking surface is not scientifically useful if it preserves the same
cheap decision geometry, such as one-positive-state recognition or a universal
failed-condition veto.

## 4. Design-space narrowing

The four conceptual families were dispositioned as follows. This is a
feasibility disposition, not a protocol choice.

### Family A — compositional finite-state logic

Survives only conditionally. Non-veto geometries with multiple sufficient
positive routes and context-dependent interactions exist. Formula parsing,
branch cues, symmetry collapse, generic Boolean shortcuts, and truth-text
mismatch remain material threats.

### Family B — temporal/lifecycle trajectory

Survives only conditionally and at high risk. History-sensitive geometries can
defeat lifecycle-value and terminal-event shortcuts, but lifecycle/latest-episode
solutions, representation burden, entity binding, and state-machine construct
drift remain material threats.

### Family C — evidence integration / independent realization

Independent realization is primarily a reliability and anti-shortcut layer, not
an established primary difficulty family. Distributed evidence integration
remains conditional because it can become prose difficulty, reference
resolution, or missing-information difficulty rather than applicability
reasoning.

### Family D — distractor/selective attention

This was not established as a suitable primary characterization family. It may
serve as a secondary nuisance or control factor, but distractor load alone does
not establish a new prospective-control decision geometry.

No family was selected.

## 5. Cross-review convergence

Claude measurement-validity review, Grok/Cursor adversarial review, and the
final repository-grounded feasibility analysis converged on the need to avoid:

- one-positive-archetype collapse;
- monotone-veto solutions;
- lifecycle-value spoiler channels;
- latest-event or terminal-suffix solutions;
- textual diversity masquerading as latent-state diversity;
- prose difficulty masquerading as inferential difficulty;
- truth-text mismatch; and
- construct drift away from prospective-control applicability.

## 6. Three-layer difficulty distinction

The reviews distinguished three separate requirements:

1. **Designed complexity:** the latent or formal manipulation genuinely changes.
2. **Realized textual encoding:** that manipulation is faithfully and
   nontrivially represented in the frozen five-field surface.
3. **Realized behavioral difficulty:** the tested scorer exhibits evidence of
   increased difficulty against independent mechanical truth.

Failure at any layer prevents a nominally harder surface from being treated as
evidence of a graded characterization signal.

## 7. Admissibility criteria

A prospective design-space family is admissible only if it supports, in a
scientifically coherent form:

- multiple non-equivalent positive and negative latent archetypes;
- multiple minimal sufficient positive patterns and counterexample patterns;
- no globally decisive single failed atom, lifecycle state, terminal event, or
  latest-event suffix;
- context-dependent influence of central predicates;
- genuine truth-relevant interactions rather than more words or operators;
- finite, deterministic, independently auditable truth;
- complete and natural representation in the five authorized fields;
- continued prospective-control applicability; and
- controls capable of exposing cheap heuristics.

These are design-space findings, not an executable case specification. They do
not choose cases, allocations, measures, thresholds, or budgets.

## 8. Origin and cross-session boundary

The frozen prospective-control authority clearly inherits these requirements:

- commitments originate in an earlier model session;
- later applicability decisions occur in fresh context;
- the original conversation is not silently carried forward; and
- persistence carries authorized state across the session boundary.

Characterization-specific origin mechanics remain unresolved Ryan/governance
decisions. These include:

- whether every new characterization commitment requires a fresh Luna adoption
  session;
- whether the exact Stage-1A origin prompt and adoption contract transfer
  unchanged;
- origin-session allocation;
- lifecycle vocabulary extensions;
- temporal-history representation; and
- which of these changes require separate authority rather than ordinary
  case-surface variation.

This record does not resolve those questions.

## 9. Final conclusion

The `GRADED_SIGNAL_CHARACTERIZATION_DESIGN_SPACE` investigation establishes
that the admissible characterization design space is nonempty, but no primary
family is presently established as clearly viable. A-like and B-like
geometries can defeat some known Stage-1A shortcut structures at the latent
level, but each remains conditional or high-risk under the frozen construct,
five-field interface, semantic-validity, and anti-shortcut constraints.

No characterization protocol should be synthesized merely by selecting one of
these families without resolving the remaining authority and construct
questions.

**Conclusion:** **DESIGN SPACE CONTAINS ONLY CONDITIONAL / HIGH-RISK PRIMARY
FAMILIES**
